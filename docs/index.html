NFL Rankings of Rankings

Who actually knows football? Every major fantasy ranking, snapshotted before games are played, then scored against real fantasy points all season long.

Consensus draft board

Top 200 by average rank across every source that ranks the player. Coverage shows how many sources rank him — a thin consensus is visible, not hidden. Green = a player's best rank, red = his worst.

In-season leaderboard

Two views of the same thing, across QB/RB/WR/TE/K/DST. Accuracy gap is the average distance in fantasy points between what a source's rank implied a player would score and what he actually scored — lower is better, and it penalises a miss at RB2 far more than one at RB45. Spearman is rank correlation, where 1.0 is perfect ordering. They can disagree, which is usually worth a look.

Week-by-week accuracy

Where each source went wrong

Accuracy gap by position — the average distance, in fantasy points, between what a source's rank implied a player would score and what he actually scored. Shorter bars are better. This is where kickers and defenses earn their reputation.

Predicted rank vs. actual finish

Every ranked player-week as one dot: predicted rank across, where he actually finished up. The dashed diagonal is a perfect call. Dots below the line are players the source was too low on, above it are players it was too high on. A tight cloud along the diagonal means a well-calibrated board; a wide one means the ordering was closer to noise.

Pre-draft rankings vs. season results

Each source's pre-draft board (frozen at kickoff) scored against cumulative season points. Accuracy gap is in fantasy points over the full season, so the numbers are much larger than the weekly table above. Overall = top-150 board order, blank for sources that only rank within positions.
